# Selling consumer electronics online in Malaysia: approvals and documents (official sources, read on 19 November 2026)

Every rule below comes from the regulator's own page or document and ends with a source tag in square brackets; the quotes are in [evidence.json](evidence.json) and checked word for word by `tools/check_all.py`. The platform's own category requirements, shown in its seller centre, are the final list for listing; this checklist is what a merchant can prepare before registering.

**Who uses this:** the category team sends it to a merchant after the first call; the merchant works through the steps; the team copies the dates into [tracker_template.xlsx](tracker_template.xlsx).

## Step 1. Which approval does each product need?

| Product type | Energy Commission approval and ST-SIRIM label | MCMC certification and MCMC label | Energy-efficiency (star) label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chargers for phones and IT equipment, portable power stations, switching power supplies | yes | only if wireless (see below) | no |
| Multiway and travel adaptors, cable reels | yes | no | no |
| LED lamps, fans, rice cookers, kettles, air fryers and other portable cooking appliances, TVs and monitors | yes | smart TVs: yes | for the types in the MEPS list |
| Phones, tablets, laptops, wireless routers and other wireless devices | no (their chargers: yes) | yes | no |
| Wireless keyboards, mice, USB dongles, wireless chargers | their plug-in adaptors: yes | yes | no |

- No one may make, import, display, sell or advertise domestic or general-public low-voltage electrical equipment unless the Energy Commission has approved it (Electricity Regulations 1994, regulation 97). [R01]
- The regulated list covers portable battery chargers up to 24 V (including chargers for mobile phones), chargers for IT equipment up to 20 V, portable power stations and switching mode power supplies. [R02]
- It also covers multiway and T-adaptors, travel and universal adaptors, and portable cable reels. [R03]
- It also covers LED lamps, fans, rice cookers, portable cooking appliances such as air fryers, kettles, and video display units such as TVs and monitors. [R04]
- Some regulated items must also meet minimum energy performance standards, among them TVs up to 70 inches, wall fans of 10 to 16 inches, LED lamps and rice cookers. [R05]
- Equipment that meets those standards carries an efficiency rating label. [R26]
- All communications equipment must be certified; the MCMC label is required on phones, tablets, computers, smart TVs, wireless routers and other wireless devices. [R06]
- MCMC's 2025 e-commerce guidelines name, among others, wireless keyboards, wireless mice, USB dongles, wireless chargers and smart TVs. [R07]
- A product that is both electrical and communications equipment needs approval from both authorities. [R08]
- Regulated accessories sold with a main product, such as a USB adaptor or charger, must meet their own standards. [R09]
- Power banks: the published list does not name them. Its nearest rows are portable battery chargers up to 24 V and portable power stations; ask the Energy Commission which row applies before listing. [R45]

## Step 2. Set up the business

- Register the business with SSM within thirty days of starting it; the registration certificate is issued within one hour of payment. [R10]
- A sole proprietorship or partnership with a trade name costs RM60 to register, and its owners must be Malaysian citizens or permanent residents. [R11]
- SSM registration does not replace the licences and approvals of other authorities. [R12]
- An Energy Commission applicant must be a company registered with SSM, with premises for storage, distribution or manufacturing; an importer must be a Malaysian company. [R13]
- SIRIM grants communications approvals only to local companies registered in Malaysia. [R14]

## Step 3. Electrical equipment: Energy Commission route

- Register as a manufacturer or importer (Certificate of Registration) on the e-DIK system. [R15]
- Documents: SSM business or company profile, warehouse tenancy agreement, local-authority business licence, photo of the premises with the signboard; there is no fee. [R16]
- The registration is valid for 1 to 5 years. [R17]
- Get an e-Permit user ID from Dagang Net (1-300-133-133), then apply for the Certificate of Approval online; a valid registration is needed first. [R18]
- Attach a type test report (at most five years old, and valid for at least twelve months from the application), the list of components, the instruction manual, the technical specification and catalogue, and a sample if asked. [R19]
- Fees: RM30 processing fee, and an annual fee of RM220 (single phase) or RM330 (three phase) on approval. [R20]
- Processing time: five working days for a new approval and one working day for a renewal, if the documents are complete. [R21]
- Imported equipment is tested by SIRIM batch by batch (consignment test); locally made equipment needs a SIRIM product certification licence. [R22]
- After the test, buy ST-SIRIM labels and fix one on each unit, on the equipment itself and not on the packaging. [R23]
- ST-SIRIM labels have carried a QR code since 10 February 2022. [R24]
- Renew the approval at least 14 days before it expires. [R25]

## Step 4. Communications equipment: MCMC route through SIRIM QAS

- In SIRIM's e-ComM system: register the laboratory, register the test report, declare the trademark or brand, then submit the compliance approval. [R27]
- Documents may be in Bahasa Malaysia or English. [R27]
- The MCMC label can be a sticker, embossed or engraved, or an e-label stored in the device's operating system. [R28]
- Importing communications equipment needs a SIRIM certificate of approval; each import approval covers one model per shipment and is valid for three months. [R29]
- Renewal fees: RM50 a year for a certificate of conformity and RM100 a year for a self-labelling ID. [R30]

## Step 5. Listing: what the product page must show

- For communications equipment, show clear evidence of certification in the product description, with a copy of SIRIM's certificate of conformity and the correct model number and brand. [R31]
- MCMC asks platforms to request proof of certification from local merchants and to verify it with SIRIM before a listing is approved, so have the certificate ready before the listing request. [R32]
- MCMC's guidelines are dated 31 July 2025; the penalty they cite for uncertified equipment is a fine of up to RM300,000, up to three years in prison, or both. [R33]
- Since 25 December 2024 (Consumer Protection (Electronic Trade Transaction) Regulations 2024), every listing must show the seller's name, website, e-mail and phone, the main characteristics, the full price with delivery and taxes, payment method and estimated delivery time. [R34]
- The seller also states that the goods meet the safety standards of the relevant agency where there are any, and the information the seller shows must be in the national language (Bahasa Malaysia). [R35]

## Step 6. Check a product before listing (public, no sign-in)

- Phones and other communications equipment: find the IMEI (dial *#06#) or serial number and enter it on recce.my or in the Check Your Label app. [R36]
- Or open eComM, choose SIRIM Enquiry, then Product Information, and type the model; a model that was never certified shows no result. [R37]
- Electrical equipment: scan the ST-SIRIM label with SIRIM QAS's mobile app, or search SIRIM's Malaysian Certified directory by company or product. [R38]
- No public page to look up an Energy Commission approval number was found; ask the merchant for the approval number and the label.

## Step 7. Cross-border sellers

- A 10% sales tax applies to goods sold at MYR500 or less and brought into Malaysia, charged since 1 January 2024. [R39]
- A seller whose low-value goods sales into Malaysia exceed MYR500,000 in 12 months must register. [R40]
- Communications equipment sold by an overseas merchant is treated as not certified for use in Malaysia, so cross-border brands of phones and wireless devices list through a Malaysian company or distributor. [R41]

## Document list for the merchant

| Document | Needed for | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| SSM business or company profile | every seller; Energy Commission registration | Set up the business; Energy Commission route |
| Warehouse tenancy agreement, local-authority business licence, photo of premises with signboard | Energy Commission registration (importers and manufacturers) | Energy Commission route |
| Type test report, list of components, manual, specification and catalogue | Energy Commission approval for each product | Energy Commission route |
| Energy Commission approval number and ST-SIRIM labels on the units | listing regulated electrical products | Energy Commission route; Listing |
| SIRIM certificate of conformity, MCMC label | listing communications equipment | MCMC route; Listing |
| Seller details, full price, delivery time, safety statement, in Bahasa Malaysia | every listing | Listing |

## Where official pages differ (use the newer one, and confirm)

- The e-Permit address is written as epermit.dagangnet.com on one Energy Commission page and epermit.dagangnet.com.my in the 2024 guidelines. [R42]
- Registration renewal opens one month before expiry on the registration page and 2 months before expiry in the 2024 guidelines; both say at least 14 days before expiry. [R43]
- MCMC's older FAQ cites a fine of up to RM100,000 or six months; the 2025 guidelines cite up to RM300,000 or three years. [R44]
