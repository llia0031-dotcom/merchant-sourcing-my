# Malaysian online consumer electronics: what public sources say (read on 19 November 2026)

Each line that states a fact ends with a source tag in square brackets; the tag's quotes are in
[evidence.json](evidence.json) and the pages are listed at the end. `tools/check_all.py` checks that every quote is
in its saved page and every number of the line is in its quotes. Figures from research firms, logistics companies
and data tools are their estimates, and statements by a platform are that platform's own statements.

**Who uses this:** a category manager deciding which sub-categories and which kinds of merchants to recruit first,
and what to say to them. The last section turns the findings into that decision.

## 1. Size: large, and growing fast

- Malaysian e-commerce reached US$20 billion (about RM83.3 billion) of GMV in 2025, growing 21% year on year, according to e-Conomy SEA 2025 by Google, Temasek and Bain. [I01]
- Momentum Works puts Malaysia among the fastest-growing markets in Southeast Asia, with 47.6% growth in 2025. [I02]
- The Department of Statistics counts e-commerce income of RM335.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 2.6% year on year; this counts income of all establishments from e-commerce, including business-to-business, so it is not online retail sales. [I03]

## 2. Where shoppers are: content commerce is now mainstream

- In an Ipsos survey of Malaysians, 62% of online shoppers said they used TikTok Shop, up from 52% in 2025 and 43% in 2024; 71% shopped on Shopee. [I04]
- Across Southeast Asia, content commerce (live, short video and content-led affiliates) made 32% of platform GMV in 2025, up from 20% in 2024, by Momentum Works' estimate. [I05]
- Nearly six in 10 users in Malaysia name live streams as their preferred way to shop, according to a TikTok platform survey. [I06]
- 76% of Malaysian shoppers say influencer recommendations affect what they buy (Airwallex study with Statista). [I07]
- Live selling does not work equally for every category, so the pitch to each merchant should be about its own products. [I39]

## 3. Price bands and best sellers

- In FindNiche's ranking of Phones & Electronics products on TikTok Shop Malaysia by recent orders, the first item is a cable protector at RM0.19. [I08]
- Other items near the top are a 120W charger with a 4-in-1 cable at RM15.0 and a handheld mini fan at RM7.49. [I09]
- EchoTik estimates that products under US$10 made 85% of units sold on TikTok Shop Malaysia in the first quarter of 2025. [I10]
- Phones sit far higher: more than 60% of smartphones in Southeast Asia are priced below US$200, while the average selling price reached a record US$349 in 1Q26, up 19% (Omdia). [I11]
- Entry prices are moving up: Xiaomi raised the Redmi Note 15 4G to RM799 from RM699 for the Redmi Note 14 4G. [I12]
- Recent Malaysian launches of mid-range and entry phones: realme 16T at RM1,399, Infinix Note 60 Pro from RM1,199, nubia A57 at RM549. [I13]

## 4. Who sells today: supply structure

- FindNiche's ranking of the 100 Phones & Electronics shops on TikTok Shop Malaysia is ordered by units sold, which favours low-priced accessories; it covers its own dataset, not all sellers. [I14]
- The shops named after brands near the top of that list are accessory brands: Mcdodo (3rd), GOOJODOQ (6th), Baseus Malaysia (14th), UGREEN (15th) and Pineng (22nd). [I15]
- A Malaysian audio brand is already in the list: Vinnfier Store at 51st. [I16]
- Larger electronics brands open their own official shops on content commerce: Xiaomi opened its official TikTok Shop store with a grand opening from 16 to 18 December 2025. [I17]
- They run the same campaign across channels: realme ran 12.12 sales across its official stores on TikTok, Lazada and Shopee. [I18]
- Several global accessory and device brands already point shoppers to an official TikTok Shop next to Shopee and Lazada (Anker, DJI, TP-Link). [I38]

## 5. Local and cross-border supply

- Since 1 January 2024, a 10% sales tax applies to imported low-value goods of RM500 or less bought online. [I19]
- It raised RM817 juta (Malay for million) in 2025, up from RM476 juta in 2024, according to the Ministry of Finance. [I20]
- The ministry says the tax helps local small and medium enterprises by narrowing the price gap between local and overseas sellers. [I21]
- Cross-border buying is common: 72% of Malaysian shoppers buy from international merchants at least once a month, and electronics is among their top three cross-border purchases (Airwallex study with Statista). [I22]
- Electronics is one of the sectors most exposed to cross-border sellers; the SME Association advises local sellers to compete on authenticity, quality, fast local delivery and after-sales support. [I23]
- TikTok reports that local MSMEs grew sales on TikTok Shop by 140% year on year, and that more than 200,000 local sellers joined in 2026. [I24]
- TikTok Shop says it allocated up to RM250 million in 2026 to support about 400,000 local merchants and creators, and announced zero commission for 56 product sub-categories. [I25]

## 6. Brands, Mall stores and playbooks

- In Malaysia brand value matters in electronics, and 43% of "savvy shoppers" put most of their spending in Mall sections (Cube Asia; Lazada and Cube Asia study). [I26]
- Nearly 30% of Malaysian consumers would pay 5-10% more to buy from a verified Mall store, according to Lazada. [I27]
- Electronics and appliances is where brand authentication matters most, by Cube Asia's reading of the marketplaces. [I28]
- TikTok Shop says its more than 100 All-Star LIVE sessions in Malaysia in 2025 gave participating Mall brands an average sales uplift of over 160% against a normal day, and that Mall brands grew 2.3 times faster than marketplace sellers. [I29]
- Smartphones (Omdia, Q3 2026 shipments): Xiaomi 21%, HONOR 18%, Samsung 16%, Apple 11%, vivo 11%. [I30]
- Shipments in Malaysia fell by more than 30% that quarter, and the five leading vendors held 77%. [I31]
- Phone brands also grow through telco channels and instalment plans such as SPayLater. [I32]
- OPPO uses zero-interest instalments of up to 24 months and periodic online sales. [I33]
- Launch live streams run in long blocks: Xiaomi's store opening ran live sessions from 2pm to 5pm and from 7pm to 12am. [I34]
- Campaign days lift higher-value electronics: in Lazada's 11.11 sale, average order value rose 76% for smart devices, 106% for computers and components and 197% for gaming devices, against the 9.9 campaign. [I35]
- Criteo reports a 155% sales increase in Malaysia on Singles' Day 2025. [I36]
- A common brand path is to start as a non-Mall seller, prove demand, then apply for Mall status. [I37]

## 7. What this means for recruiting

- **Bring in brands that sell higher-value electronics well elsewhere.** Shoppers are already on content commerce (section on where shoppers are), but the electronics leaders there by units are low-priced accessory sellers (sections on price bands and supply). Brands with official stores on Shopee, Lazada or their own sites, and no content-commerce shop yet, are the gap the pool targets.
- **Lead with local brands and distributors.** The low-value-goods tax narrows the price gap for local sellers, shoppers value authenticity and after-sales in electronics, and the platform is funding local merchants. A local brand with Malaysian warranty and approved products is the strongest first call.
- **Make the Mall case with evidence.** Brand trust matters most in electronics; the published Mall results and the hybrid path (non-Mall first, then Mall) give a merchant a step-by-step reason to start.
- **Plan the first month around campaign days and live streams.** Campaign days lift order values in smart devices, computers and gaming; the first-month plan in `coldstart/` puts the first live stream in the second week.

## Gaps

- No public source gives Malaysia-only GMV shares for each platform, the share of local against cross-border sellers, or an electronics share of content commerce in Malaysia.
- The shop rankings come from one third-party dataset and rank by units sold; they say nothing about margins or brand ownership.
